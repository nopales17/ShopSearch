## Test discipline
For each test, record dataset/store, time window, metric, baseline, minimum sample/coverage, success and failure thresholds, cost/time budget, owner, and allowed next action before observing results. Unless specified below, numeric thresholds and samples are pending and block that test's gate decision, not Issue #1. Inadequate traffic/data is inconclusive. Link actual findings from EVIDENCE; do not backfill favorable thresholds or treat a successful research artifact as production promotion.

| H2a: display scan labor (CHARTER; S1 §§1,2,3,12) | One display photo yields many useful actual-product crops at lower total human effort than manual entry. | H2c: repeated physical state (S1 §3.4) | Repeated photos maintain useful identity/freshness with low correction labor. Label a repeated-display dataset including moves, occlusion, duplicate-looking objects and removals; measure false merges/splits/removals and correction effort. | Apparent disappearance is regularly mistaken for removal or sold state; manual burden stays high. | Limited reconciliation after evaluated error bounds; never infer sale merely from absence. |
| H2d: evidence fusion (S1 §§1,3) | Barcode/product data, invoice and POS inputs improve specific identity/price/state claims at lower cost than vision alone. Compare one available source pair against verified records, preserving conflicts. | Incorrect links, stale data or integration labor erase gains. Receiving/invoice data does not prove current stock. | Only the demonstrated adapter and claim policy; no universal fusion engine. |
| H3: intent value (CHARTER; S1 §4) | Queries and interaction patterns add merchandising information beyond sales-only data. After adequate catalog coverage and outcome access, compare scoped patterns with owner/sales baselines and small tracked probes. | Effects disappear after controlling repeated sessions, search defects, coverage, seasonality or attribution; no economic outcome improvement. | Bounded intent reports/experiments, not unit-demand estimates or purchasing authority. |
| H4: evidence-aware decision value (CHARTER; S1 §5,11,14; S2 SER context) | Consequence-aware policies improve decisions/probes relative to owner, naive sales/intent heuristics and LLM-only baselines. Freeze time-correct inputs and evaluate replay/simulation/shadow cases with cost, regret, abstention/probe quality and outcomes. | No improvement, excess probe cost, leakage from future outcomes, or gains only under simulator assumptions. Replay cannot establish unobserved counterfactual outcomes. | Bounded shadow/probe evaluation first; consequential action needs separate authorization and stronger support. |

H4 preserves provenance/scope/freshness and distinct support, amplify, damp, inhibit, defeat roles as research semantics, not a generic confidence number. Future traces should distinguish evidence available at decision time, policy/model version, proposed/authorized action, limitations, probe and later outcome. Do not implement an evidence graph, SER runtime or trace engine now. ACT / PROBE / STOP from S2 and INSUFFICIENT / WATCH / PROBE / RECOMMEND in S1/contracts are unresolved policy vocabularies; no equivalence or executable authority is assumed.

## Conflicts, overclaims and reconciliation
- S1 p.1 labels market facts “verified”; this repository has not verified them. The 96,786-establishment figure is a dated adjacent-population claim, not TAM, eligible customers, or willingness to pay. SMB/enterprise AI survey interest does not establish this customer's need.
- S1 §§6–7 calls primitives validated and assigns defensibility ratings. Advertised capabilities are not independent performance or demand validation. “We are collapsing” catalog labor (§7.1) must remain “we are testing whether”; no moat is established.
- S1 §2.3's progressive tiers mix image quality, identity, metadata and stock support. They are not an authority ladder: better photos do not strengthen stock truth; a barcode identifies a product type, not necessarily an individual physical item; invoice/POS links support only particular timely claims.
- S1 §4.1 says zero results means no suitable match. Strictly it means retrieval returned none under that query/filter/index/policy. Suitable catalog items may have been missed. A nonempty result list also does not establish suitability or physical availability.
- S1 §5.1 lists short dwell time as supporting evidence. Its meaning is ambiguous; neither short nor long dwell proves demand. S1 §6.1/16 implies every store/event improves the loop; noisy or biased data may not help.
- S1 §§2,9,11 and Appendix C include image uploads/live display demos in the early experience. Current production is curated text-to-image retrieval only. Sales demos must disclose preparation and experimental capabilities; never imply a curated catalog came from live automated scanning.
- Appendix C mixes “last updated” and photographic freshness. Import/publication time is not observation time. Stronger structured sources also need scope/freshness validation before “in stock” wording.
- S1 §11 has a paid-site Phase 0 exit and subjective Phase 1 gate. Repo Phase 0 remains technical prerequisites; commercial acceptance is tracked separately; fixed retrieval and instrumentation gates remain.
- A plausible UX explanation for weak use earns a UX experiment, not production vision. Weak incremental search does not alone falsify useful browsing. Customer value and catalog-labor savings are separate gates.
- S1 §5 permits autonomous hero changes conceptually. It is not authorization for a production agent or external actions. Model proposals never become inventory truth or purchasing authority by themselves.
- S1 §13 identifies regulated-category/platform concerns. Its statements are not accepted current legal advice. Store location, merchandise and deployment-specific requirements must be resolved before public launch; no claim that non-transactional owned sites are automatically exempt.

## Long-horizon context only
“High-entropy inventory” means assortments whose appearance, uniqueness, variants or churn make conventional cataloging costly; it is a working segmentation idea, not a universal ontology. Potential adjacent domains include thrift/vintage/consignment, antiques, jewelry, art, gifts, furniture/decor, plants, collectibles and boutiques. No fixed expansion order or success is assumed.

S1 pricing scenarios: $750–$1,500 setup, $99–$149/month Starter, $199–$249 Growth, $499+ multi-location; none is an accepted offer or validated willingness to pay. $199/location revenue arithmetic excludes costs, churn and acquisition; it is not a forecast. Recurring-charge timing is an experiment, not a requirement to wait for automation.

S1 proposes $650,000 for ~18 months, illustrative allocations (45% product, 25% GTM, 15% data, 10% legal/security, 5% contingency), 25–50 paying stores and two verticals. These are fundraising scenarios, not an approved budget, hiring plan, roadmap deadlines or an instruction to bypass gates. Competitive names/prices and cited market statistics stay in the dated source until independently checked for a concrete use.

Exception-only owner interaction, accumulated decision traces, operational autonomy and generalizing SER beyond retail remain research directions. Cross-store data access/permission, outcome attribution and consequential-action authority must be designed only when a bounded caller exists. The white paper is not required reading for ordinary implementation.
